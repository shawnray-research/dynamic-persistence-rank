# ACM Transactions on Algorithms (TALG): submission facts

Sources: dl.acm.org/journal/talg/author-guidelines (via search snapshot; the page itself returns 403 to bots), dl.acm.org/journal/talg/reviewers, Wikipedia (TALG).
* Submission: ManuscriptCentral, http://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/talg. Create an account, choose "Transactions on Algorithms", upload the
  manuscript (PDF) plus source files.
* Scope: "original research papers of lasting value" on the mathematical aspects of discrete algorithms. Referee criteria: (1) the paper
  is among the best of the year in its area of algorithmics; (2) it interests the algorithms community; (3) the presentation is clear and
  effective.
* Review model: referees are anonymous; no anonymization of submissions is required (single-blind). Author names on the PDF are fine.
* Format: final versions must use the ACM style files (acmart, journal format acmsmall). There is no page limit; full proofs belong in the
  paper.
* Prior publication: TALG also publishes journal versions of conference papers (e.g. invited SODA papers). An arXiv preprint is fine
  under ACM's prior-publication policy. Post to arXiv at submission time; it is the single best lever for citations.
* Open access: ACM is completing its transition to full open access (2026), so an article processing charge may apply unless the
  author's institution participates in ACM Open (CMU is expected to). Check before final acceptance.
* Statistics: in 2024 TALG received 169 original submissions and accepted 41 (about 24%).

## Cover letter draft (about 150 words)
Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We submit "Dynamic Betti Numbers Are Equivalent to Dynamic Sparse Matrix Rank" for consideration in ACM Transactions on Algorithms.
The paper settles, conditionally, why no known method updates the homology of a changing simplicial complex in sublinear time.
It proves that dynamic Betti numbers and dynamic matrix rank are equivalent up to constant factors. From this it derives OMv-based lower
bounds that hold over every field, for bounded-degree complexes, for Vietoris-Rips complexes of changing point sets, for online zigzag
persistence, and even for deciding whether the first homology vanishes. It complements these with polylogarithmic algorithms for
connectivity and for subcomplexes of the 3-sphere. The results connect dynamic algorithms, fine-grained complexity and computational
topology, communities that TALG serves.
An earlier version was submitted to SODA 2027. All reviewers found the results interesting but asked for a substantially clearer
presentation, which this version provides.
Suggested handling area: data structures / computational geometry and topology. Possible referees (no conflicts known): experts on
dynamic algebraic algorithms and on computational topology.

Sincerely, Shawn Ray
